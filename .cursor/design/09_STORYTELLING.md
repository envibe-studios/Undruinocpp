# Storytelling

Undruinocpp is not a story about heroes.

It is a story about civilization.

The player witnesses humanity through the lives of ordinary people living extraordinary circumstances.

Stories should reveal the character of humanity rather than simply advance the plot.

---

# Story Emerges Through Play

The strongest stories are the ones players create themselves.

Scripted narrative should support emergent storytelling rather than replace it.

The most memorable moments should happen because of player decisions, teamwork, improvisation, and coincidence.

---

# Every Expedition Tells A Story

Every mission should answer at least one question.

Players should leave knowing something they did not know before.

About:

- humanity
- another faction
- an alien civilization
- themselves
- the universe

Even small discoveries contribute to the larger narrative.

---

# Show, Don't Explain

Prefer environmental storytelling.

Players should discover stories through:

- abandoned machinery
- architecture
- journals
- symbols
- damage
- repairs
- ruins
- artifacts

Dialogue should deepen understanding rather than deliver exposition.

---

# Every Civilization Has A Voice

Ruins should feel authored.

A player should recognize that another civilization once lived here.

Not merely that "aliens existed."

Architecture.

Tools.

Art.

Writing.

Rituals.

Infrastructure.

Everything should hint at who they were.

---

# Questions Create Wonder

Mystery is often more valuable than answers.

Not every question should be resolved.

Allow players to speculate.

Allow competing interpretations.

Some mysteries should survive until the end of the game.

---

# Every Faction Tells The Same Story Differently

No discovery has one interpretation.

An Administrator...

A Returner...

A Pilgrim...

A Reliquarian...

...should all see the same event differently.

The player should understand why.

---

# History Is Layered

History is rarely complete.

Records conflict.

Memories fade.

Myths distort truth.

Players should occasionally discover that accepted history was incomplete rather than entirely false.

Truth is assembled piece by piece.

---

# Small Stories Matter

Not every memorable story changes civilization.

A child's drawing.

A maintenance log.

A wedding photograph.

A broken instrument.

A memorial plaque.

These remind players what humanity is fighting to preserve.

---

# The Universe Is Older Than Humanity

Players should constantly feel that humanity arrived late.

Ancient civilizations.

Forgotten worlds.

Ruined megastructures.

Lost technologies.

Silent observatories.

The universe should always suggest deeper history beyond what players currently understand.

---

# Hope Survives

Even in tragedy, stories should preserve hope.

Humanity's defining characteristic is not survival.

It is the determination to continue despite uncertainty.

Hope should never disappear entirely.

---

# Storytelling Test

When writing a mission or narrative moment, ask:

Does this deepen the player's understanding of humanity?

Does this create new questions?

Does this reward curiosity?

Will players discuss this after the mission ends?

If not, reconsider the narrative.
