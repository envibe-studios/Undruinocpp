# Factions

The factions of Undruinocpp are not defined by power.

They are defined by hope.

Every faction exists because it offers a different answer to humanity's oldest question:

"How do we save our people?"

No faction should view itself as evil.

Every faction sincerely believes it is acting in humanity's best interest.

Understanding a faction should always precede judging it.

---

# Shared Origins

All factions descend from the same civilization.

They share:

- history
- language
- technology
- ancestry
- hardship

They disagree about humanity's future.

Not humanity's value.

---

# Philosophy Before Politics

Factions are philosophies first.

Organizations second.

Political structures emerge from their beliefs.

Not the other way around.

Members should be identifiable by the values they express more than the uniforms they wear.

---

# Humanity Comes First

Even opposing factions generally want humanity to survive.

Conflicts arise because they disagree on:

- what humanity needs
- where humanity belongs
- what sacrifices are acceptable
- who should lead
- what should be preserved
- what should be abandoned

---

# Every Faction Is Incomplete

Every philosophy solves some problems.

Every philosophy creates others.

No faction possesses the complete truth.

No faction is entirely mistaken.

The player should understand the appeal of every faction before encountering its weaknesses.

---

# The Administrators

Core Belief:

Humanity survives through continuity.

Mission:

Preserve civilization.

Maintain the fleet.

Protect future generations.

Continue searching for a new home.

Strengths:

- stability
- organization
- institutional knowledge
- infrastructure
- long-term thinking

Weaknesses:

- bureaucracy
- slow adaptation
- resistance to radical ideas

Core Question:

"How do we ensure humanity survives another century?"

---

# The Returners

Core Belief:

Humanity abandoned its home too quickly.

The stories of exile have become mythology.

No living person truly knows what waits behind them.

Mission:

Return.

Learn the truth.

Either reclaim the old home or finally discover why it was truly lost.

Strengths:

- courage
- skepticism
- independence
- willingness to challenge accepted history

Weaknesses:

- romanticizing the past
- accepting enormous risks
- questioning institutions others depend upon

Core Question:

"What if everything we've believed is wrong?"

---

# The Pilgrims

Core Belief:

No planet can become home until humanity is accepted.

The true exile is spiritual rather than physical.

Mission:

Seek understanding.

Seek forgiveness.

Seek a higher purpose.

Attempt communication with whatever greater intelligence governs the universe.

Strengths:

- compassion
- patience
- philosophy
- diplomacy
- long-term perspective

Weaknesses:

- inaction
- idealism
- willingness to delay practical solutions

Core Question:

"What if home is not a place, but a relationship?"

---

# Additional Factions

Future factions should continue this pattern.

Each should answer a different question about humanity's future.

Avoid creating factions whose only purpose is conflict.

Every new faction should emerge from a sincere desire to protect humanity.

---

# Cultural Expression

Factions should differ through:

traditions

rituals

architecture

music

clothing

language

celebrations

education

symbols

attitudes toward technology

rather than simple color palettes or military equipment.

Culture is more memorable than uniforms.

---

# Internal Diversity

No faction is monolithic.

Members disagree.

Leaders disagree.

Generations disagree.

Individuals interpret philosophy differently.

Avoid portraying entire factions as sharing identical beliefs.

---

# Relationships

Factions should cooperate as often as they compete.

Trade.

Research.

Celebrate.

Argue.

Mourn.

Intermarry.

Share technology.

Civilization survives because complete isolation is impossible.

---

# The Player

Players should rarely encounter purely evil people.

Instead, they should encounter good people making difficult decisions from different philosophical perspectives.

The most memorable choices occur when every option carries understandable consequences.

---

# Faction Test

W# The Humanity Test

When writing a faction member, ask:

Would a reasonable player understand why this person believes what they believe?

Would a reasonable player consider joining this faction?

Can this character sincerely explain why their philosophy gives humanity the best chance to survive?

If the answer is no, the faction has probably become too one-dimensional.

Every faction also looks for answers in a different place.

Some look toward the future.

Some look toward the past.

Some look toward the divine.

Some look toward civilizations that came before.

Where a faction searches often defines it more than what it believes.
