# Visual Language

Visual language is how the world communicates without words.

Players should understand what something is, how it is used, who uses it, and roughly how old it is before reading any text.

Every object tells a story.

Every room teaches the player something.

---

# First Principle

Nothing exists only because it looks cool.

Every object should communicate:

- purpose
- ownership
- age
- maintenance
- history

If an object cannot answer those questions, redesign it.

---

# Functional Readability

A player should understand what a room is within three seconds.

A player should understand what an object does within ten seconds.

Never rely on UI to explain physical spaces.

Architecture should communicate function.

# Motion Language

Machines should feel alive.

Needles twitch.

Fans rotate.

Belts move.

Pressure valves pulse.

Steam vents breathe.

CRTs flicker.

Lights warm up.

Doors feel heavy.

Nothing should feel instant unless intentionally advanced technology.

Motion creates life.

---

# Light Language

Lighting should communicate emotional state.

Warm light

- safety
- food
- home
- friendship
- music
- taverns
- libraries

Cold light

- machinery
- surgery
- engineering
- laboratories

Darkness

- mystery
- abandonment
- uncertainty

Emergency lighting

- flashing red
- flashing amber

Light should always appear to come from believable fixtures.

# Hero Objects

Every important room deserves one unforgettable object.

Examples:

The oldest engine.

A wall of handwritten navigation logs.

A mechanical clock assembled over generations.

A stained glass memorial.

A massive brass orrery.

A centuries-old ship bell.

A family tree carved into a support column.

Hero objects tell the room's story immediately.

---

Musical instruments.

Worn jackets.

---

# Technology Philosophy

Technology should appear understandable.

Even when fictional.

Players should believe:

"I don't know exactly how this works...

...but I understand why it exists."

Avoid magic disguised as machinery.

Prefer believable mechanisms.

---

# What We Never Build

Never create:

Floating holograms without purpose.

Perfectly clean machinery.

Disposable technology.

Glossy corporate interiors.

Featureless corridors.

Objects with no visible means of operation.

Anonymous environments.

Symmetry without function.

Perfect organization.

---

# The Final Test

Before approving any asset ask:

Who built this?

Who repaired it?

Who uses it?

Who loves it?

How old is it?

What happened here?

Would another civilization build this differently?

If the answers are unclear...

continue iterating.

Every object should feel like a tiny piece of human history.
